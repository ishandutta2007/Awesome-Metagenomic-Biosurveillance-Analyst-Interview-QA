# Topic 05: Wastewater & Environmental Surveillance

## Overview
Wastewater-based epidemiology (WBE) design, the SARS-CoV-2 surveillance precedent, signal interpretation, and extension to broader pathogen portfolios.

---

### Q1: What did the SARS-CoV-2 WBE experience teach us, and what are its key advantages and limitations?

**A:**
**Validated findings from COVID-19 WBE:**
1. **Lead time advantage:** WBE signals preceded clinical case increases by 4-7 days — individuals shed SARS-CoV-2 in feces before symptom onset; asymptomatic infections contribute to WBE but not clinical counts; testing system delays further lag clinical surveillance.
2. **Population coverage independent of testing rates:** Captures all infected sewershed contributors regardless of healthcare-seeking or testing access — robust to clinical surveillance underdetection during testing overwhelm.
3. **Variant tracking:** Detected emerging variants (including Omicron) before clinical genomic surveillance, providing population-scale genomic epidemiology at lower per-capita cost.
4. **Resource efficiency:** Single WWTP sample serving 100,000 people provides population-level burden estimates equivalent to thousands of individual tests.

**Key limitations:**
1. **Sewershed spatial resolution:** WWTP-level WBE attributes signal to entire sewershed — cannot localize to specific neighborhoods or buildings without subcatchment sampling.
2. **Quantitative interpretation uncertainty:** Requires accurate population denominators and flow volumes; precipitation-driven dilution; per-capita shedding rate variability.
3. **Shedding rate variability:** Individual fecal shedding varies by orders of magnitude across disease stages, limiting prevalence estimation precision.
4. **Not all pathogens detectable:** Works best for fecally-excreted pathogens; limited for exclusively blood-borne or respiratory-only transmission.

### Q2: Design a multi-pathogen wastewater surveillance program extending beyond SARS-CoV-2.

**A:**
**Priority pathogen tiers:**

Tier 1 (high priority, established feasibility): Influenza A/B; Norovirus GI/GII; Poliovirus; Mpox; RSV

Tier 2 (emerging priority): H5N1 avian influenza; Enteroviruses (EV-D68, EV-A71); Hepatitis A/E; AMR organisms

**Technical challenges in multiplex WBE:**
1. **Extraction/concentration optimization:** Different pathogens require different approaches (enveloped vs. non-enveloped viruses; gram+/- bacteria); no universal method; parallel processing streams add complexity.
2. **Multiplexed RT-qPCR vs. metagenomics trade-off:** Targeted RT-qPCR provides high sensitivity, quantitative output, same-day results for defined targets but misses novel pathogens. Metagenomics enables untargeted detection but lower sensitivity, higher cost, longer turnaround. Pragmatic approach: targeted RT-qPCR for established priority targets + periodic metagenomics for novel pathogen surveillance.
3. **Cross-pathogen normalization:** Different fecal shedding concentrations, wastewater stability (degradation half-lives: hours to weeks), extraction efficiencies — normalization approaches (crAssphage, pepper mild mottle virus, synthetic spike-ins) must be validated per pathogen target.

## Summary
COVID-19 WBE validated wastewater surveillance as a cost-effective, leading-indicator public health modality — multi-pathogen expansion requires careful prioritization by epidemiological burden and WBE technical feasibility, with extraction optimization, multiplexed detection, and cross-pathogen normalization as the primary technical challenges.
